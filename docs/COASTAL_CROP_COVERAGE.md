# Coastal Syrian crop catalogue — first evidence-backed expansion

## Scope and evidence

This is a first catalogue expansion, not a claim that every crop or local cultivar in the coastal region has been enumerated. The crop presence list is grounded primarily in the Syrian Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform's *Agricultural Investment Map of the Syrian Arab Republic* (prepared in 2021, using 2020 agricultural statistics), specifically the Tartus and Latakia governorate sections.

Primary source:
- https://acsad.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/الخارطة-الاستثمارية-الزراعية-في-الجمهورية-العربية-السورية-2022.pdf

Useful source sections:
- Tartus greenhouse crop list and field crops: pages 102–106 (including protected tomato, cucumber, pepper, eggplant, beans, strawberry, banana, mango, custard apple, dragon fruit, zucchini; tobacco, potato, maize, pulses, oil crops and winter vegetables).
- Tartus fruit trees: pages 107–108 (apple, pear, quince, citrus types, almond, peach, nectarine, cherry, greengage, walnut, fig, apricot and other fruits).
- Latakia greenhouse and crop sections: pages 121–124 (citrus, olive, tobacco, greenhouse vegetables, potato, tomato, beans, cowpea, maize, vegetables, oil crops and fruits).

Recent local reports add emerging or limited coastal plantings:
- SANA, March 2026, reports avocado, mango and papaya cultivation in limited areas of Tartus and Latakia: https://sana.sy/en/economic/2304189/
- Al-Wehda, December 2023, reports greenhouse and crop patterns in Latakia including avocado and kiwi: https://alwehda-news.sy/الحمضيات-والزيتون-أهم-زراعاتها-89950-هكتاراً-ضمن-اهتمام-دائرة-زراعة-منطقة-اللاذقية/

## What this catalogue does and does not mean

- A crop entry means that cultivation in the coastal governorates is documented or reported; it does not prove equal prevalence across every coastal village.
- The 2021 atlas uses 2020 statistics. Emerging crops such as avocado, kiwi and papaya are marked as limited/emerging rather than traditional major crops.
- Newly added crops have empty `commonDiseaseIds`. This is deliberate: no disease has been mapped to a crop without reviewed evidence.
- Temperature and irrigation fields for new entries explicitly say that locally reviewed values are not yet available. No generic values were invented.
- The offline camera remains coarse image screening, not a trained disease classifier.
- Weather early-warning remains available only for the existing grape downy mildew indicator. No rules are inferred for other crops from grape thresholds.
- This catalogue is not a complete national crop census and should be expanded with local agricultural directorate records, crop calendars and expert review.

## Review status

The catalogue is a crop-presence index only. Disease associations, symptom references, locally appropriate phenology, irrigation advice, weather thresholds and treatment guidance require separate evidence review before publication as recommendations.
